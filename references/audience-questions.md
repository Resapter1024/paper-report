# Audience questions and rehearsal

Use for $paper-report questions or a natural-language request to anticipate questions or rehearse Q&A. Work from the current slides and actual script, including a draft. Inspect existing files before requesting them. If only one artifact is available, use it and state the coverage; if neither is available, request presentation materials rather than inventing a talk from the paper.

Use the audience background already agreed. When unspecified, assume classmates who have heard the talk but have not read the source and do not have deep knowledge of the paper or field. Do not restart planning for a self-contained questions request.

Follow the talk's sequence. Ground each question in a visible claim, example, equation, figure, or spoken explanation. Questions may connect pages, but must not require a result, appendix, or specialist term available only in the source or notes. A source can verify an answer; it cannot supply assumed listener knowledge.

Prioritize plausible questions about meaning, motivation, assumptions, the procedure, evidence, or an application of a stated result. Include skeptical follow-ups when the talk invites them. Avoid generic examinations, leading questions advertising the method, forced objections, and questions that pretend a clear explanation was absent.

By default, give a concise numbered list in the conversation, with the displayed slide number/title (or a labeled physical PDF page) and a brief reason tied to what was shown or said. Choose coverage and depth to suit the talk without padding. Provide answers only when requested. Do not edit slides/scripts or save a separate question file unless asked.

When answers are requested, start with a short oral answer, followed by optional technical detail and relevant limits. Verify consequential answers against the available source or analysis and distinguish supported conclusions from conjecture. Save Q&A in notes when requested; do not automatically add it to the talk.
